The gap is real. Here is what creates it.

The problem is not the degree. The degree premium is still significant and growing in many fields. The problem is that most graduates finish school with a degree and nothing else. No certifications, no specialized skills, no documented work in their field. In a labor market where employers have more candidates than ever, the degree alone no longer differentiates. What differentiates is what you added on top of it.

What works

What successful graduates did differently

Across fields and degree types, the graduates who beat underemployment shared a consistent set of behaviors. None of them required switching majors or spending more money on school.

2x

One relevant certification

Graduates with even one industry-recognized certification were twice as likely to land a field-aligned role within 6 months of graduation compared to those without.

+$11K

Internship or work experience

Graduates with at least one internship or documented field experience earned an average of $11,000 more in their first job than those without, across all major categories.

67%

Started building skills early

Two thirds of graduates who beat underemployment started adding credentials before their junior year. Starting senior year is possible but cuts the time to apply skills significantly.

3.4x

Specialized within their field

Graduates who chose a specialization inside their major (a niche, an industry, a tool set) were 3.4 times more likely to be employed in their field than those who stayed general.

The degree still matters. Only the degree no longer does.

College graduates earn 65% more over a lifetime than non-graduates on average. The gap is not that college does not work. The gap is that college alone no longer works. Every tool on PathDecider is designed to help you close that gap before you graduate, not after.
